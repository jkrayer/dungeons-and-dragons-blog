Since the end of my last game I've been thinking a lot about the pace of combat, sessions and campaigns. Wile I strongly prefer sandbox style games where outcomes are dictated solely by player choice I'm slowly warming the the idea that taking some responsibility for tempo is important to better myself as a <abbr title="Dungeon Master">DM</abbr>.

This realization has been jumbled up over the past few weeks with thoughts about my player survey results, dice systems, <abbr title="Role-Playing Game">RPG</abbr>s and more. I'm left with questions.

- Is the d20 system too pass/fail?
- Is it fun to miss rolls?
- Should I run <abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr> since my players like it<sup>[1](#footnote-1)</sup>?
- Can I run a <abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr> I actually enjoy?
- Can <abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr> combat with 7 players fit into a 10 minute round?<sup>[2](#footnote-2)</sup>
- Do I want to continue developing my own setting?

I went into the session before last of Jeff's game with all of this on my mind. That night I watched Bryan fail every roll. As a fellow player I was on edge since Bryan's failures and successes impact the party's survival. As a <abbr title="Dungeon Master">DM</abbr> all I could think about was how frustrating it must be for Bryan to miss all night.

I think this is an experience I could only have as a player. It's influenced my thinking and research for the week prior to this post.

Since the pace of sessions and campaigns comes down predominantly to how I think about and prepare games I'll address those elements elsewhere. The rest of this article is about the speed of combat.

The speed of combat comes down to two factors. How complex are the rules? How quickly can the players and <abbr title="Dungeon Master">DM</abbr> execute their turn given those rules?

For the sake of the rest of this discussion let's grant that we'll continue to play some flavor of <abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr> which means that on each round of combat the player may hit or miss and that hitting is more fun than missing. Given these assumptions the variable we'll want to control for is time. How often will the player have the opportunity to roll and hopefully hit. Lets call this Time To Next Attack, <abbr title="Time to Next Attack">TTNA</abbr>.

Let's also assume the ideal length of time for the execution of a single combat round is <= 10 minutes<sup>[2](#footnote-2)</sup> making our maximum <abbr title="Time to Next Attack">TTNA</abbr> 10. With 7 players each would need to take their turn in about 1 minute leaving the <abbr title="Dungeon Master">DM</abbr> with 3 minutes to handle the monsters.

Is this possible in <abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr>?<sup>[1](#footnote-1)</sup> Maybe<sup>[3](#footnote-3)</sup>. Under the above ideal conditions a <abbr title="Time to Next Attack">TTNA</abbr> 10 combat lasting 10 rounds would take an 1 hour and 40 minutes. Ouch!

Considering the conditions of my last <abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr> game-<abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr> RAW using Fantasy Grounds with 7 players-a <abbr title="Time to Next Attack">TTNA</abbr> 10 seems breakneck. Unfamiliarity with Fantasy Grounds, technical issues, the complexities of <abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr> and the need for enough monsters to challenge 7 players leads to rounds closer to <abbr title="Time to Next Attack">TTNA</abbr> 15 - 20. That's 2.5 to 3 hours for a 10 round combat. Double ouch!

Here I had to ask myself, "How can I make this go faster?" I remembered videos I'd seen in the past about this and turned to Dungeons Craft for advice.<sup>[4](#footnote-4)</sup>

For those of you not inclined to watch the videos I'll sum up. If you cut a bunch of rules out of <abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr> and add some cool extra dice combat plays much faster. It sounds fun.

But if there's something I learned about myself over the course of writing this blog it's that I prefer systems that I can add to instead of systems I have to subtract from. So instead of running out to the store for some fresh sharpies to fix the problems in the <abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr> PHB I'm going to look at achieving a <abbr title="Time to Next Attack">TTNA</abbr> 10 in other <abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr>s.

I ran my last game using A<abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr> and did my best to do initiative as described in my article [<abbr title="Advanced Dungeons and Dragons">AD&D</abbr> for 5E Players: Combat, but Mostly Initiative](https://sage-jim.com/2023/06/24/add-for-5E-players-combat-but-mostly-initiative/). A typical round started with me asking, "Archers are you going to shoot, fighters are you going to swing, casters are you going to cast?". It may seem onerous to set up but but after you play with the same group for a while it's fairly quick. I think it's fair to say we averaged a <abbr title="Time to Next Attack">TTNA</abbr> 10 during that game.<sup>[6](#footnote-6)</sup> Which is still an 1 hour and 40 minutes for a 10 round combat.<sup>[5](#footnote-5)</sup>

And still that' not fast enough for me.

A few years back I ran Caves of Chaos for some friends using the Basic rules. Initiative in Basic is simple. Roll d6 for both sides, high roll goes first, ties go simultaneously. Rounds go in the same order each time; Movement, Missiles, Magic, Melee. When I ran those combats (in person) I'd simply say, "You need to hit AC 5," and let the players go at the same time.

I think we can agree that if a human can process 2 or 3 players rolling dice at the same time software will handle simultaneous rolls at least as efficiently. Given this I think a <abbr title="Time to Next Attack">TTNA</abbr> as low as 5 is quite possible.

Modern <abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr> certainly provides the players a lot of fun options but the exercising of those options comes at a cost in time. When it comes to controlling the pace of combat I can't see a better way of making it faster than moving to a simpler <abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr>.

## My Answers

- _Is the d20 system too pass/fail?_ <sup>[6](#footnote-6)</sup> **Maybe**
- _Is it fun to miss rolls?_ **No**
- _Should I run <abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr> since my players like it?_ **Maybe**
- _Can I run a <abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr> I actually enjoy?_ **No**
- Can <abbr title="Fifth Edition">5E</abbr> combat with 7 players fit into a 10 minute round?(1) **No**
- Do I want to continue developing my own setting? **Maybe**
