The results are 62.5% in, and I'd like to share them together with my conclusions. I'll revise the numbers in this post when the response increments to 100%. Something I did differently this time is take the survey and (I think) configure it so you can all see the results. I am the outlier in certain areas, so I will call myself out by name but everyone else's responses will of course remain private.

If you read the [Campaign Retrospective Results](/blog/campaign-retrospective-results/) article you'll find this article<sup>[1](#one)</sup> shorter. This is due mostly to the fact the many of the questions were designed<sup>[2](#two)</sup> to be verification questions. In other words a lot of questions about styles of game were asked in an attempt to reveal any inconsistencies in our group's tastes. I don't think any meaningful amount of inconsistency was revealed.

## Abstract

The group so far has an 85% positive response to <abbr title="Fifth Edition Dungeons and Dragons">5E</abbr> excluding my vote. Including me, it drops to 76%. I flatter myself to think I know they players well enough to think that when the final results come in (again excluding me) the result will hold at 85% or better.

As briefly as possible I dislike <abbr title="Fifth Edition Dungeons and Dragons">5E</abbr> because the rules create a style of game that I don't enjoy preparing, running or playing. I fumble through that conclusion in far more words elsewhere on this blog<sup>[3](#three)</sup> and since it's been coming up again among the YouTube DMs I follow I may revisit the subject with more clarity. I can't blame you if that's far more detail than you're interested in.

Given the above my best dispassionate advice for the group is to drop me and find someone who wants to play or run <abbr title="Fifth Edition Dungeons and Dragons">5E</abbr>. Enjoy yourselves, I won't be offended.

If for some reason you feel compelled to keep playing with me read on.

## Survey in Appropriate Detail

The response to the question _"If you've played any of these TTRPGs rate them 1 to 5. Skip them if you haven't played them."_ is as follows. Note, I've excluded all games with < 4 responses. That threshold was taken as there not being enough input to make a group wide decision. All results include my vote.

| Game                                                                | % Positive | No. of Votes |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------ |
| Mörk Borg                                                           | 80%        | 4            |
| <abbr title="Fifth Edition Dungeons and Dragons">5E</abbr>          | 76%        | 5            |
| <abbr title="Second Edition Advaned Dungeons and Dragons">2E</abbr> | 75%        | 4            |
| <abbr title="Advaned Dungeons and Dragons">AD&D</abbr>              | 68%        | 5            |

Mörk Borg is the most popular game we've played so we should consider playing again in the future. Our group has grown since last time so we'd probably want the expanded classes to eliminate duplication. In light of the group's general like of complex combat systems I personally doubt it's viability as a long term game system but clearly it's great fun to dip into.

I think the rest of the results speak for themselves.

Most of the rest of the questions in the "General Refresher" section were about gauging what people like as a means to influence the types of future games I may run, but there were a few interesting results.

For the question _"Rate these types of table-top games (1 to 5)"_ (board, war and rpg) board games received the highest ranking and war games the lowest. Significant in light of <abbr title="Fifth Edition Dungeons and Dragons">5E</abbr> being the most liked system. Its combat basically being a board-war game.

For the question _"Rank these styles of game"_ received rankings of 3-5 except for myself who ranked this style 1. This is a curious result since I've seen some amount of the players become immediately annoyed when I railroad.

Most of the "Combat Specifics" questions were centered around determining whether the player's investment matched their likes. This section was very consistent with two things worth mentioning.

There is a high-expectation among the players that the software will _"track my character's advantage and disadvantages."_ I'm sorry to disappoint you. The software is limited.

> <abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr> games that offer more character customizations tend to put powers and modifiers under the direct control of players. In these cases magic items tend to be fewer and their pluses smaller

Most players ranked this question at equal balance but the question was far too vague since it makes no assertion about what balance is. It may surprise you to learn that <abbr title="Fifth Edition Dungeons and Dragons">5E</abbr> doesn't expect players to start gaining uncommon magic items until some time after 5th level and rare items until some time after 11th. In other
words players should expect only find Potions of Climbing or Healing or Scrolls with cantrips or 1st level spells until about 5th level. +1 items, scrolls with 2nd to 3rd level spells, many potions and minor wands would start turning up after level 5.<sup>[4](#four)</sup> I've yet to meet a player who likes this.

Conversely, an <abbr title="Advaned Dungeons and Dragons">AD&D</abbr> adventure is likely to provide potions, scrolls and +1 weapons and armor during the first adventure.

Regarding the rest of the survey it's no surprise that you all understand (at least mostly) the rules and that we have reasonable and mature expectation of each other.

I think this clocks in at half the length previous post so, you're welcome.

While writing I had more thoughts about editions that you can think of as bonus content like deleted scenes at the end of a DVD. You know, those shiny plastic coasters we used before you could watch everything digitally.

## Editions

I have some pretty strong opinions about <abbr title="Tactical Studies Rules">TSR</abbr> era <abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr> vs <abbr title="Wizards of the Coast">WOTC</abbr> era <abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr> but I'm far more interested in discussion<sup>[5](#five)</sup> than hard positions. No I won't run or play <abbr title="Wizards of the Coast">WOTC</abbr> <abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr>, but I view <abbr title="Tactical Studies Rules">TSR</abbr> era <abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr> more as a starting point that can be modified to taste rather than as a set of rules that must be strictly followed.

The real barrier to "home-brewing" is the software.

### TSR Era D&D

Going on 2 years ago I had more than a few private "struggle sessions" over just what <abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr> I wanted to play after leaving <abbr title="Fifth Edition Dungeons and Dragons">5E</abbr> behind. I "penned" my conclusion in the article "[Picking A Forever Game](/blog/picking-a-forever-game/)" but for the sake of transparency I'll sum up below.

A few notes before the summation. I'm not wed to <abbr title="Advaned Dungeons and Dragons">AD&D</abbr>. My observations of the group in action was that the group is mostly casual players which made 5E too complex a system<sup>[6](#six)</sup>. The factors I struggled over were, availability on VTTs, availability of printed materials, availability of retro-clones and generic feels.

#### Second Edition Advanced Dungeons and Dragons

This is the edition I played most as a kid and therefore the edition I have the most nostalgia for.

##### Pros

1. I have the materials on Fantasy Grounds
2. Nostalgia
3. Many Settings
4. Reasonable cost on [DriveThru](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/16868/player-s-handbook-revised-2e)

##### Cons

1. Steep cost of [original printed books](https://www.ebay.com/b/Dungeons-Dragons-Players-Handbooks/158712/bn_1913775).
2. DriveThru only has the revised edition.
3. One little known retro-clone, "[For Gold and Glory](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/156530/for-gold-glory)"
4. I disliked nearly all of the "Complete" books.
5. There were too many rules I wanted to tear off the system.

#### Advanced Dungeons and Dragons

This is the first edition I learned to play. I have a great fondness for many of this editions modules.

##### Pros

1. Nostalgia
2. Classic Settings
3. Reasonable cost on [DriveThru](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/17003/players-handbook-1e?src=hottest_filtered)
4. [OSRIC](https://osricrpg.com/)
5. [Adventures Dark and Deep](https://www.brwgames.com/category/adventures-dark-and-deep/)
6. [Hyperborea](https://www.hyperborea.tv/).
7. Indy publishers still write modules for this game.

##### Cons

1. Steep cost of original printed books (see above).
2. Gygaxian Prose, but OSRIC.
3. Combat charts.
4. An amount of quality of life rules changes but fewer than <abbr title="Second Edition Advaned Dungeons and Dragons">2E</abbr>.

#### Basic Dungeons and Dragons

The term basic covers (if you can believe it) 3 "sub" editions of the Dungeons and Dragons game. For the sake of brevity I tend to think of it as the [BECMI-Mentzer](https://waynesbooks.games/2022/06/09/becmi-dungeons-dragons-1983-90-basic-expert-sets-fully-revised-by-frank-mentzer/) edition but my prose and cons list covers the [B/X-Moldvay](https://waynesbooks.games/2022/04/21/b-x-dungeons-dragons-1980-1-basic-expert-sets-side-by-side/) edition due to the retro-clone [Old School Essentials](https://necroticgnome.com/pages/about-old-school-essentials).

##### Pros

1. Simplicity
2. Available on Fantasy Grounds
3. [Accessible and Affordable](https://necroticgnome.com/products/old-school-essentials-advanced-fantasy-players-tome)
4. New Adventures being published
5. Easily compatible with <abbr title="Tactical Studies Rules">TSR</abbr> modules
6. Advance rules remove "class as race"
7. Active zine expanding the rules
8. The [Dolmenwood](https://necroticgnome.com/pages/about-dolmenwood) game.
9. The [OSE SRD](https://oldschoolessentials.necroticgnome.com/srd/index.php/Main_Page)
10. There are just a lot of retro-clones of B/X

##### Cons

1. Too much simplicity?
2. No real nostalgic connection
3. Limited levels
4. Some sunken cost on "<abbr title="Dungeons and Dragons">D&D</abbr>" (foolish I admit)
